## 4. Header analysis

### 4.1 Identity fields

| Field | Value (defanged) | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| `From` | `IT Support <it-support@meridian-fin[.]example>` | Displays the organisation's own domain |
| `Reply-To` | `mailbox-admin@meridian-support[.]invalid` | Different domain from `From` |
| `Return-Path` | `bounce-4417@mx1.relay-ns2[.]invalid` | Third, unrelated domain |
| `Message-ID` | `<20260114.084012.4417@mx1.relay-ns2[.]invalid>` | Generated by the envelope host, not the displayed domain |
| `Date` | `Wed, 14 Jan 2026 08:40:12 +0000` | Consistent with the first trusted `Received` hop |

Three different domains across `From`, `Reply-To` and `Return-Path` is the core observation.
It is not by itself proof of anything — ESPs and ticketing systems produce similar splits —
but `Reply-To` pointing at a *lookalike* of the organisation's own name is a different matter
from `Reply-To` pointing at a recognised ESP.

### 4.2 Authentication

`Authentication-Results` recorded by the receiving gateway:

```
Authentication-Results: mx.meridian-fin.example;
    spf=fail (sender IP is 203.0.113.77) smtp.mailfrom=relay-ns2.invalid;
    dkim=none;
    dmarc=fail action=quarantine header.from=meridian-fin.example
```

Independent verification of the displayed domain's own published records
(`evidence/case-001-dns.txt`, queried 2026-01-14T09:02Z):

```
meridian-fin.example.  TXT  "v=spf1 include:_spf.corp-mail.example -all"
_dmarc.meridian-fin.example.  TXT  "v=DMARC1; p=quarantine; rua=mailto:dmarc@meridian-fin.example; adkim=s; aspf=s"
```

**Interpretation of each result, stated precisely:**

- **SPF fail** — the sending IP is not authorised for the *envelope* domain
  (`relay-ns2.invalid`). Note what this does and does not mean: SPF evaluates
  `smtp.mailfrom`, not the `From:` header the user sees. A message can pass SPF and still
  spoof the visible sender entirely.
- **DKIM none** — no signature present. Absence is not forgery evidence on its own; plenty of
  legitimate mail is unsigned. It does, however, remove the one mechanism that could have
  survived forwarding and proved authorship.
- **DMARC fail, `p=quarantine`, strict alignment** — this is the load-bearing result. DMARC
  evaluates whether an authenticated identifier *aligns* with the `From:` domain. With
  `aspf=s`, an envelope domain of `relay-ns2.invalid` cannot align with
  `meridian-fin.example` under any circumstances.

**Why this is not a single-indicator conclusion:** three mechanisms with different failure
modes agree. If the sender were a legitimate but misconfigured ESP, the expected pattern
would be SPF pass on an ESP envelope domain plus an aligned DKIM signature — the exact
opposite of what is observed.

### 4.3 Received chain

Read bottom-up (oldest hop first). Hops above the first trusted host are attacker-assertable.

| # | Hop (defanged) | IP | Timestamp (UTC) | Trust |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (oldest) | `from localhost by webmail-04.relay-ns2[.]invalid` | — | 08:40:10 | **Untrusted** — asserted by the sender |
| 2 | `from webmail-04.relay-ns2[.]invalid by mx1.relay-ns2[.]invalid` | `203.0.113.77` | 08:40:12 | **Untrusted** |
| 3 | `from mx1.relay-ns2[.]invalid ([203.0.113.77]) by mx.meridian-fin[.]example` | `203.0.113.77` | 08:40:19 | **First trusted hop** |
| 4 (newest) | `by mbx-03.meridian-fin[.]example` | internal | 08:40:21 | Trusted, internal |

**Findings:**

- The **injection point** is hop 3: the first hop written by infrastructure the organisation
  controls. `203.0.113.77` is the only sending IP that can be relied upon; everything below
  it is the sender's own claim.
- Hop 1 asserts `localhost`, consistent with a script or webmail component submitting
  directly on the sending host. This is a common pattern for bulk senders of all kinds,
  legitimate and otherwise.
- Hop latency (2s, 7s, 2s) shows no unusual delay — no evidence of store-and-forward through
  an intermediate queue.
- No `Received` hop references `corp-mail.example`, the provider authorised in the
  organisation's SPF record. A genuine internal notification would traverse it.

### 4.4 Body and pretext

| Observation | Note |
|---|---|
| Urgency: "within 24 hours or your mailbox will be deactivated" | Time pressure reduces verification behaviour |
| Authority impersonation: signed "IT Support" | Internal-authority pretext |
| Generic salutation "Dear User" | Argues against tailored targeting |
| Single embedded link, anchor text `Verify mailbox` | Anchor text differs from href |
| Href | `hxxps://mailbox-verify.relay-ns2[.]invalid/auth/login?u=dXNlci0wNDE3` |
| Parameter `u=` | Base64 of `user-0417` — the recipient identifier |

The `u=` parameter is significant twice over: it shows the sender possessed recipient-specific
data, and it is a **victim-unique token** that must not be submitted to a public sandbox. It
is redacted in all published material here.

a blocklist will block the organisation's own IT Support address. Spoofed identity is an
indicator of the *pretext*, not of the *adversary*.

## 7. Assessment

The message is assessed to be a **credential-phishing attempt impersonating internal IT
support**, delivered by infrastructure unauthorised for the displayed domain.
**Confidence: High** — three independent authentication mechanisms agree, the routing chain
contains no hop through the organisation's authorised mail provider, and the message body's
link destination is unrelated to the displayed sender.

No assessment is offered as to who operates `relay-ns2[.]invalid`. Nothing in this artefact
supports attribution, and the infrastructure has not yet been investigated (Cases 002–004).

## 10. Analyst note

The tempting conclusion at minute two was "the domains don't match, therefore phishing".
That reasoning would also flag a large share of legitimate ESP, ticketing and newsletter
traffic. The conclusion here rests instead on DMARC alignment failure under a strict policy
combined with the absence of the authorised provider anywhere in the routing chain — a
pattern legitimate mail from this domain cannot produce.
